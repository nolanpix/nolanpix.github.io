RTL2GDS: synthesis is the easy half

I keep an eye on the RTL2GDS leaderboards the way some people check sports scores. The headline number is always "it synthesized" - and that is the easy half. Synthesizing RTL into a gate-level netlist is a solved-enough problem. Getting a clean, timing-closed GDSII out the far side is where most flows actually fall apart.

Where flows break

The gap between "synthesized" and "signed off" is a graveyard of specific failures:

  • Setup and hold that pass in isolation but fight each other after CTS.
  • Routing congestion that only shows up once the clock tree is real.
  • DRC and antenna violations that the netlist never hinted at.
  • Timing that closes at the typical corner and explodes at the slow one.

Each of these lives in the physical domain, not the logical one. You cannot see them from the RTL. They are the reason the back end is a job and not a button.

What "timing-closed" actually asks

Timing closure is not one check; it is the whole chain holding at once:

  • WNS and TNS clean across every corner and mode, not just the easy one.
  • No hold fixes that quietly re-open setup somewhere else.
  • A clock tree whose skew and insertion delay the timer actually believes.
  • Signoff parasitics, not estimates - the numbers change, sometimes a lot.

The habit

So when a new flow lands a clean GDSII with timing closed, it is worth more than a faster synthesis time. Synthesis gets you to the start line. Everything interesting - and everything hard - happens between placement and signoff.
