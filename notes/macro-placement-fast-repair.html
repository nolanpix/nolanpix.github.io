Macro placement: fast local repair beats raw wirelength

I spent an evening reading through a winning run of a macro-placement challenge, and the takeaway was not a clever cost function. It was discipline.

Spend search, not wirelength

The instinct on macro placement is to chase total wirelength: pack the macros, shrink the nets, call it done. The run that won did something less glamorous and more effective: it ran a multi-start search, kept many candidate floorplans alive, and ranked them with a congestion-aware score rather than pure wirelength.

A placement that looks short on paper and routes like a swamp is not a good placement. Congestion is the real currency.

Fast local repair is the engine

The other half was a cheap, fast local repair step applied over and over. Instead of trying to produce a perfect global arrangement in one shot, you take a decent one and nudge it: shove a macro out of a hotspot, open a channel, re-check. Because each repair is cheap, you can afford thousands of them across the multi-start pool.

  • Generate many starts; do not trust a single seed.
  • Rank by congestion and routability, not wirelength alone.
  • Repair locally and often; keep the loop tight.

Legality is not optional

The part that is easy to skip and expensive to skip: strict legality checks on every candidate. Overlaps, off-grid macros, blocked rows - if an illegal placement slips into your ranking, you will optimize toward a fantasy and lose the hours it takes to notice. Check legality up front, reject early, and only score what could actually tape out.

None of this is exotic. It is the same lesson the back end keeps teaching: the clever cost function matters less than a fast loop, an honest score, and a hard legality gate you never turn off.
